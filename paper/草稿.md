## Introduction
Large language models (LLMs) have achieved strong performance in open-domain dialogue, instruction following, and general text generation. However, role-playing dialogue generation remains a challenging setting. Unlike general conversational tasks, role-playing requires a model not only to produce relevant and natural responses, but also to consistently maintain a target character’s persona, tone, values, and narrative perspective across multiple turns. For such applications, users often care not only about whether a response is reasonable, but also whether it sounds like something the character would actually say.

A common approach to role-playing is prompt-based persona conditioning, where a character card or persona description is provided in the system prompt. This approach is simple to implement, relatively inexpensive to deploy, and often effective in eliciting character-like responses. However, prompt-based methods alone are often insufficient for stable role-playing. Models may fall back to generic assistant-style language, produce meta discourse, or drift away from the intended persona. In multi-turn interactions, they may also show identity inconsistency, weak memory continuity, or repetitive generation. In other words, prompting can activate role-playing behavior, but it does not necessarily sustain it reliably.

In this context, parameter-efficient fine-tuning offers a practical direction for role-playing adaptation. Compared with full fine-tuning, LoRA and its quantized variant QLoRA enable task adaptation with substantially lower computational and memory cost, making them suitable for small-model customization under limited resources. This leads to a natural question: when a base model already exhibits some role-playing ability with the help of a character card, can LoRA fine-tuning further improve role expression and consistency? If so, where does the improvement mainly appear? If not, are the main limitations caused by parameter adaptation itself, or by other factors such as data quality, context modeling, and evaluation design?

Answering this question is not simply a matter of training a LoRA model for role-playing. In practice, the final quality of a role-playing system is jointly affected by prompt design, training data format, supervision strategy, and evaluation settings. These factors are often intertwined, making the actual contribution of fine-tuning difficult to isolate. For this reason, it is useful to examine role-playing adaptation under controlled comparisons, so that the effects of persona prompting and parameter-efficient fine-tuning can be observed more clearly.

Motivated by this consideration, we conduct LoRA-based role-playing experiments on `Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct` using character dialogue data under a resource-constrained setting. We compare several configurations under matched conditions. For evaluation, we combine automatic metrics with `LLM-as-Judge` assessment, and examine model behavior from several aspects, including role adherence, multi-turn consistency, repetition, and refusal tendency.

**This work does not aim to propose a new role-playing method. Instead, through controlled comparative experiments, we study how prompt conditioning, parameter-efficient fine-tuning, data format, and evaluation choices jointly affect role-playing performance in practice, and we summarize several empirical observations from the results.** Our experiments suggest that LoRA fine-tuning can improve single-turn role expression to some extent and reduce generic assistant-style responses, while problems such as multi-turn inconsistency and repetitive generation remain far from resolved. These findings indicate that improving small-model role-playing quality cannot be attributed to parameter adaptation alone, but depends on the joint effect of data quality, context modeling, and evaluation design.

近年来，大语言模型在开放域对话、指令跟随与文本生成任务中取得了显著进展，但角色扮演式对话生成仍然是一个较具挑战性的场景。与一般对话任务不同，角色扮演不仅要求模型生成语义相关、表达自然的回复，还要求其在多轮交互中持续维持特定角色的人设、语气、价值取向与叙事视角。对于这类应用而言，用户关注的往往不只是回答是否合理，也在于回答是否符合角色设定，并具有一定的连贯性与沉浸感。

目前，基于 `character card` 或 `persona prompt` 的提示式方法是角色扮演中较常见的实现路径。这类方法实现简单、部署成本较低，并且通常能够在一定程度上激活模型的角色表达能力。然而，仅依赖 prompt 往往难以稳定支撑较复杂的角色扮演过程。模型容易回到通用助手式表达，出现元话语、拒答或风格漂移；在多轮对话中，也常出现身份不一致、记忆断裂与重复生成等现象。换言之，prompt 能够提供角色扮演的初步条件，但未必足以支持更稳定的角色保持。

在这一背景下，参数高效微调为角色扮演建模提供了一个较为现实的方向。相比全参数微调，LoRA 及其量化变体 QLoRA 能够以较低的计算和显存成本完成任务适配，因此更适合资源受限条件下的小模型定制。由此引出一个自然问题：当基础模型已经借助角色卡具备一定角色扮演能力时，LoRA 微调是否仍能进一步改善角色表达；如果可以，这种改善主要体现在哪些方面；如果有限，其影响因素又来自模型适配本身，还是来自数据、上下文建模与评测方式等其他环节。

回答这一问题的困难并不只在于训练一个角色扮演 LoRA 模型。更关键的是，角色扮演系统的最终表现往往同时受到 prompt 设计、训练数据形式、监督目标与评测设置的共同影响，而这些因素在实践中通常相互交织，使得参数微调的实际作用并不容易直接判断。因此，相比单纯关注某一训练环节，更有必要在尽量一致的条件下进行对比分析，以更清楚地观察 prompt 与参数微调各自带来的变化。

基于这一考虑，本文以 `Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct` 为基础模型，结合角色对话数据，进行了面向资源受限场景的 LoRA 微调实验，并在统一条件下比较了多种设置下的模型表现。评测中，本文结合自动指标与 `LLM-as-Judge` 方法，从角色贴合度、多轮一致性、重复率以及拒答倾向等维度进行观察与分析。

本文的重点不在于提出新的角色扮演方法，而在于通过分组控制的对比实验，分析角色扮演实践中由 prompt、参数微调、数据形式与评测方式共同带来的影响，并据此给出若干基于实验结果的经验性观察。实验结果表明，LoRA 微调能够在一定程度上改善单轮角色表达，并减少通用助手式回复；但与此同时，多轮一致性与重复生成等问题仍未得到明显改善。这说明，小模型角色扮演能力的提升并不能简单归结为一次参数适配，而仍然依赖于数据质量、上下文建模与评测设计等因素的共同作用。

## Background
#### 1. Role-Play Dialogue Generation
Role-play dialogue generation is a conditional generation task centered on a predefined character. Compared with general instruction-following tasks, it requires the model not only to produce responses relevant to user input, but also to consistently reflect the target persona in wording, tone, values, and narrative perspective. In multi-turn settings, the model is further expected to maintain relatively stable character identity and memory continuity as the dialogue develops.

The difficulty of this task mainly lies in three aspects. First, character information is usually provided in the form of a character card or persona description, and the model needs to translate these descriptions into stable generation behavior. Second, multi-turn dialogue continuously introduces new context, so persona information, dialogue history, and current user input may compete with one another, especially under limited context windows. Third, role-play quality is inherently open-ended: a single reference answer is often insufficient to determine whether a response truly sounds like the target character, which makes standard reference-based metrics less adequate for evaluation.

#### 2. Limitations of Prompt-Based Persona Conditioning
A common approach is to control the base model through a persona prompt or character card. This method does not require additional training, has relatively low deployment cost, and can often produce noticeable improvements in short role-play conversations.

However, this approach also has limitations. Base models are typically biased toward general assistant behavior, and their default safety language, explanatory style, and neutral phrasing may conflict with role-playing objectives. At the same time, prompts operate only at inference time and do not change the model’s underlying task preference, so their effect may weaken as the context becomes longer. When the target persona has a strong speaking style or the multi-turn setting becomes more complex, prompting alone is often insufficient to maintain stable role consistency.

#### 3. LoRA and QLoRA
LoRA is a commonly used parameter-efficient fine-tuning method. It freezes most pretrained parameters and introduces low-rank update matrices into selected linear layers, allowing task adaptation with relatively low training cost. Under limited computational resources, this offers a practical balance between efficiency and experimental flexibility.

QLoRA further combines LoRA with low-bit quantization by loading the base model in 4-bit form while keeping LoRA parameters trainable, which substantially reduces memory usage. For the `Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct` model used in this work, QLoRA makes multiple role-play fine-tuning experiments feasible on consumer-grade GPUs.

Still, the trainability of LoRA does not directly imply improved role-play quality. For this task, the more relevant question is whether fine-tuning actually improves role expression, consistency, and common failure patterns, rather than merely reducing training loss.

#### 4. Problem Setting of This Work
This work focuses on a specific question: when a character card is already provided as a strong conditioning signal, can parameter-efficient fine-tuning further improve role-play performance in small models? **In this study, LoRA is treated as a testable adaptation method rather than a guaranteed solution.** The following method and experiments are designed to examine its practical effect on role expression, multi-turn consistency, and common failure patterns.

####  1. 角色扮演对话任务 
角色扮演对话生成是一类面向特定人物设定的条件生成任务。与一般指令跟随任务相比，它不仅要求模型给出与用户输入相关的回复，还要求模型在表达中持续体现角色身份，包括措辞、语气、价值取向和叙事视角等方面的一致性。对于多轮对话而言，模型还需要在不断变化的上下文中维持相对稳定的人设与记忆连贯性。

 这一任务的难点主要体现在三个方面。首先，角色信息通常以角色卡或人物设定文本的形式给出，模型需要将这些描述转化为稳定的生成行为。其次，多轮对话会不断引入新上下文，角色设定、对话历史和当前输入之间容易发生竞争，有限上下文窗口下尤其容易出现遗忘或漂移。最后，角色扮演质量具有较强开放性，单一参考答案难以充分刻画“是否像这个角色在说话”，因此仅依赖传统参考式指标往往不够。 

#### 2. Prompt-based Persona Conditioning 的局限 
目前较常见的做法是通过 persona prompt 或 character card 对基础模型进行条件控制。这种方法不需要额外训练，部署成本较低，也常常能够在较短对话中带来较明显的角色表达效果。 但这类方法也存在边界。基础模型的默认分布通常偏向通用助手，其安全表达、解释性语言和中性措辞有时会与角色扮演目标发生冲突。与此同时，prompt 只是在推理阶段施加条件，并未改变模型内部的任务偏好，因此其控制效果可能随着上下文变长而减弱。当角色风格较强或多轮设定较复杂时，仅靠 prompt 往往难以维持稳定一致的表现。 

#### 3. LoRA 与 QLoRA 
LoRA 是一种常用的参数高效微调方法。它冻结大部分预训练参数，仅在部分线性层上引入低秩增量矩阵，从而以较小的训练开销实现任务适配。对于资源有限的实验场景，这种方法能够在训练成本和实验灵活性之间取得较好的平衡。 

QLoRA 在此基础上进一步结合低比特量化，将基础模型以 4-bit 形式加载，同时保留 LoRA 参数的可训练性，从而显著降低显存占用。对于本文使用的 Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct 而言，QLoRA 使得在消费级 GPU 上进行多组角色扮演实验成为可能。

不过，LoRA 的可训练性并不直接等同于角色扮演质量的提升。对于这类任务，更值得关注的是微调是否真正改善了角色表达、一致性和错误模式，而不仅仅是训练损失的变化。

#### 4. 本文的问题定位 
本文关注的是一个较为具体的问题：在角色卡已经作为强条件输入存在的情况下，参数高效微调能否进一步改善小模型的角色扮演表现。围绕这一问题，本文将 LoRA 视为一个可检验的适配手段，而不是预设有效的答案。后续的方法设计与实验分析，主要用于观察 LoRA 在角色表达、多轮一致性和常见错误模式上的实际作用。
